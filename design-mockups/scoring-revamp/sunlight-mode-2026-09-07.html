Live Scoring · Readability

Sunlight Mode

Players can't read the keypad or the card on a bright fairway. Dark mode is the wrong tool outdoors — it leaves most of the glass sitting at the reflection floor, where the sky and your own face land on top of the thin text. Below: both scoring views as they are today, next to a proposed third mode. Drag the glare slider — it puts a reflected-light veil over every phone at once, which is the only way to judge this indoors.

design-mockups/scoring-revamp/sunlight-mode-2026-09-07.html · real #scorecardActiveSection + #tradCardView CSS
Glare 45% · hazy sun ≈ 40,000 lux on the glass. Reflected light adds a constant luminance floor to every pixel, so measured contrast collapses toward 2:1 and colour stops being the lever — stroke mass does.

#scorecardActiveSection · SCV3

The active-player band, the four group cards and the number keys are what a scorer actually aims at between shots. Today all three lean on hairline borders, translucent panels and Outfit at weight 200–300 — the first things glare erases.

touch_app These three are live. Tap the number keys — the band and the green highlight advance to the next player exactly as saveCurrentScore does on the course, stepping over the red “their phone” card. Drag the glare slider while you tap: the question is whether the band is still findable once it moves.

Why the fix is weight, not colour

Reflected ambient light adds the same luminance to every pixel on the glass. That floor compresses whatever contrast the design started with, and it compresses a 21:1 ratio and a 5:1 ratio toward the same place. Numbers below are for a 600-nit phone at roughly 40,000 lux with ~4% screen reflectance — an overcast Pattaya morning, not even direct midday.

Indoors
18.3:1

#10151B on white. The ink was never the problem — today's light theme measures fine on a desk.

At 40,000 lux
≈2.2:1

Same two colours. Everything on the screen lands in the same narrow band, so hue and ratio stop separating things.

What still survives
Mass

Stroke weight × size, and solid fills instead of tints. An 800-weight 34px numeral reads at 2.2:1; a 300-weight 26px one does not.

Why not dark
Specular

A dark screen sits at the reflection floor across most of its area, so the sky and your face mirror back exactly where the thin light text is.

Two consequences drive every change above. Translucent panels stop existing — a 0.09-alpha border has almost no delta from its ground once the floor is added, so every panel edge becomes a solid 2px line. And tints stop reading as state — a 13% green wash behind the active player is invisible, which is why the active card, the name band and END all become solid fills with white text. The app already learned this twice: the rank badges went solid in July ("gradients and glows washed out in sunlight"), and TRADV's selected cell has been solid #16a34a since it shipped. Sunlight mode is that same rule applied everywhere instead of in two places.

How it would ship

  1. A third state on the toggle that already exists. DECIDED ThemeMode becomes Light → Dark → Sun instead of Light → Dark. Every [data-theme-toggle] button in the app gains it for free, icon wb_sunny. Sun sets theme-sun alongside theme-light, so all existing light rules keep applying and the sun layer only overrides what's listed above.
  2. Plus a SUN pill in the live-scoring chrome. Nobody hunts for a theme toggle standing over a putt. In SCV3 it goes in .scv3-hole-actions — that row already wraps, so it costs no width at 360px. In TRADV it mirrors into the legend row, the same slot the pin indicator and the starting-tee switch took.
  3. Scoped to the round, remembered per player. Sun only makes sense on #golferDashboard.round-active. Stored the same way the colour theme already is, so it survives a hole-to-hole reload but doesn't follow anyone into the clubhouse.
  4. One CSS block, no JS beyond the toggle. Same DOM, same class names, no render path touched. It has to sit at 4-ID specificity like the TRADV colour shield, or the classic light-round rules will repaint it.
  5. Verified at 360px before it goes near a fairway. Everything here grows — 44px keys become 52px, 15px cells become 19px. The keypad column still has to clear one phone screen without scrolling, and the TRADV strip still has to hold one row.
Still open Does the solid green active-player band read right to you, or is it too heavy next to the rest of the app? That's the one call left — Sun ships as a third mode alongside Light and Dark, and the card's top strip keeps every element on one line exactly as it is today.
